The problem

Work your team keeps doing by hand

The software may work well on its own. Moving information between tools often takes more time than it should.

between systems

Copy, paste, repeat

Your team keeps copying the same data between systems. Orders, products, prices, inventory and supplier information move by hand between SaaS platforms, spreadsheets, PDFs and internal tools.

in files and memory

Data that lives in someone’s head

Important business data lives in spreadsheets, PDFs, websites or people’s memory. The information exists, but the organization cannot use it consistently.

on the website

The website says one thing, the shelf another

Prices, stock and images online drift away from what you actually sell. Supplier price lists change, image links break, and products go live without prices — and nobody can fix thousands of listings by hand.

in the core system

A core system you’ve outgrown

Your POS, ERP or SaaS works — but not for the way you work now. Replacing it looks risky and expensive; keeping it means employees do the same workaround every day.

between teams

Each department has a different record

Sales, purchasing and the warehouse keep separate records. Staff have to check which version is current before they can act.

document handling

Someone has to read every file

Supplier PDFs and spreadsheets arrive in different formats. We can help extract the information, match it to your records and flag uncertain results for review.

Who it’s for

For teams working with orders, stock and supplier data

If your business runs on products, orders, inventory, suppliers and customers — and your team spends part of every day moving that information between systems, spreadsheets and PDFs — this is for you.

These projects often start with a spreadsheet, a price list or a task that takes too much of someone's day.

Typically teams of 5 to 200 people, where operations are too complex for spreadsheets alone but too specific for an off-the-shelf platform to fit perfectly.

You will probably recognize at least one of these

  • A supplier sends a new price list, and someone spends days updating it.
  • The website, the POS and the spreadsheet each show a different stock number.
  • One person knows where everything is — and everything stops when they are away.
  • Your core system works, but everyone does the same workaround every day.
  • You are thinking about switching systems, and afraid of what will break.

Method

From the current process to a tested solution

  1. 01

    Understand

    See how the business actually operates: roles, systems, repeated actions, data sources, decisions, exceptions.

  2. 02

    Rebuild

    Decide what to automate, what stays human, what data needs structure, and where systems connect.

  3. 03

    Build

    An automation, an internal tool, an integration, a data pipeline, an AI-assisted workflow, or a website operation.

  4. 04

    Integrate

    Work with the systems already in place — or migrate to a new one when the operating model requires it.

  5. 05

    Validate

    Test against real cases: exceptions, human review, source checks, and read-back reconciliation.

How we check the work

  1. Rule 1

    Measure, don't assume.

    An image is “working” only after a real request loads it — not because the file name looks right.

  2. Rule 2

    Every write can be undone.

    Bulk changes run in preview first and ship with a rollback.

  3. Rule 3

    Disable, don't delete.

    Deleting breaks links and history; hiding does not.

Existing systems

Start with the software you have

We usually start by improving the workflow around your existing ERP, POS, SaaS, website, spreadsheets, legacy desktop software and supplier systems.

When the operating model has outgrown the core system, we help you switch — without losing the business rules, data and habits that made the old one work.

Sometimes a small tool is enough. Sometimes the core system needs to change. We work out which applies before choosing the software.

People and systems

Agree on what staff need to review

Tools can prepare data and carry out agreed rules. Pricing, purchasing and uncertain matches still need a named person to review or approve them.

Systems do

  • Collect, move, format and convert data
  • Match, look up and consolidate
  • Repetitive entry and bulk updates
  • Rule execution and verification
  • Drafts, diagnostics and recommendations

People decide

  • Ambiguous cases
  • Business exceptions and approvals
  • Pricing and purchasing decisions
  • Customer-specific situations
  • Final accountability

Depending on the job, we use APIs, scripts, document extraction or a small internal web tool. AI can help read files and suggest matches; it does not approve prices or purchases.
