Who it’s for

For teams held together by spreadsheets

If your business runs on products, orders, inventory, suppliers and customers — and your team spends part of every day moving that information between systems, spreadsheets and PDFs — this is for you.

These projects often start with a spreadsheet, a price list or a task that takes too much of someone's day.

Typically teams of 5 to 200 people, where operations are too complex for spreadsheets alone but too specific for an off-the-shelf platform to fit perfectly.

You will probably recognize at least one of these

  • A supplier sends a new price list, and someone spends days updating it.
  • The website, the POS and the spreadsheet each show a different stock number.
  • One person knows where everything is — and everything stops when they are away.
  • Your core system works, but everyone does the same workaround every day.
  • You are thinking about switching systems, and afraid of what will break.

Five steps. Tested before it goes live.

Understand the work, build a small trial, check the result and hand it over.

  1. 01

    Understand

    See how the business actually operates: roles, systems, repeated actions, data sources, decisions, exceptions.

  2. 02

    Decide what to automate, what stays human, what data needs structure, and where systems connect.

  3. 03

    Build

    An automation, an internal tool, an integration, a data pipeline, an AI-assisted workflow, or a website operation.

  4. 04

    Integrate

    Work with the systems already in place — or migrate to a new one when the operating model requires it.

  5. 05

    Validate

    Test against real cases: exceptions, human review, source checks, and read-back reconciliation.

How we check the work

  1. Rule 1

    Measure, don't assume.

    An image is “working” only after a real request loads it — not because the file name looks right.

  2. Rule 2

    Every write can be undone.

    Bulk changes run in preview first and ship with a rollback.

  3. Rule 3

    Disable, don't delete.

    Deleting breaks links and history; hiding does not.

How we work →

Machines do the repeats. People make the calls.

See which actions a tool can carry out and which still need approval.

Systems do

  • Collect, move, format and convert data
  • Match, look up and consolidate
  • Repetitive entry and bulk updates
  • Rule execution and verification
  • Drafts, diagnostics and recommendations

People decide

  • Ambiguous cases
  • Business exceptions and approvals
  • Pricing and purchasing decisions
  • Customer-specific situations
  • Final accountability

Depending on the job, we use APIs, scripts, document extraction or a small internal web tool. AI can help read files and suggest matches; it does not approve prices or purchases.
